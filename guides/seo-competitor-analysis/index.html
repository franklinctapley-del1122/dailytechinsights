SEO COMPETITOR RESEARCH

SEO Competitor Analysis: What It Is & How to Do It

Learn how to study competing search results, keywords, content, links and technical patterns to find opportunities for your own SEO strategy—without copying competitors.

Published: September 18, 2026 · Last reviewed: September 18, 2026 · By DailyTechInsights Editorial Team · See our methodology
SEO competitor analysis is the process of studying websites and pages that compete for the same search demand so you can understand their strengths, gaps, content patterns and opportunities. A useful analysis can include search intent, ranking pages, keyword coverage, content structure, backlinks, internal linking, technical patterns and SERP features. The goal is to learn what the search results reward and identify a better, more relevant strategy—not to reproduce another site.
Start with the SERP: your true SEO competitor may not be your business competitor. For a particular query, the competing results can include publishers, directories, marketplaces, documentation sites and other resources.

1. Identify Your Real SEO Competitors

Choose competitors based on the queries and topics you care about. Search a representative set of terms and record the domains and pages that repeatedly appear. Then separate direct business competitors from search competitors.

Competitor typeMeaningWhy it matters
Business competitorA company competing with you commercially.Useful for market positioning and product research.
Search competitorA page or domain competing for the same query.Useful for SEO content and SERP analysis.
Topic authorityA strong publisher covering the topic broadly.Useful for understanding depth, entities and topical coverage.

2. Analyze Search Intent

Look at the pages that rank for the query and ask what task they help the searcher complete. Compare format, depth, examples, comparisons, tools, images, tables and calls to action.

Do not assume that the highest-ranking format is the only format that can work. Search intent is the starting point; your page still needs a clear reason to be useful.

3. Find Keyword and Topic Gaps

Keyword gap analysis compares the queries where competitors appear with the queries where your site is missing or weaker. The objective is not to publish a page for every missing keyword. Group related queries into real topics and decide whether an existing page should be improved or a genuinely new resource is needed.

Our Keyword Research for SEO guide covers intent, metrics and prioritization in more detail.

4. Study Competitor Content

Compare the structure and usefulness of competing pages. Look for recurring sections, missing explanations, original research, examples, tools, FAQs and decision criteria.

Content signalWhat to investigate
CoverageWhich important subtopics are consistently addressed?
DepthWhere do competitors provide evidence, examples or practical detail?
Original valueDo they offer data, screenshots, tests, frameworks or unique analysis?
UsabilityIs information easy to scan, compare and act on?
FreshnessWhich claims, products or workflows require ongoing updates?

5. Analyze the SERP Itself

Study the search results page, not just the competing articles. Note the presence of ads, local packs, videos, images, featured snippets, discussions, product results or other search features when they appear.

SERP analysis can reveal whether the query is primarily informational, local, commercial or mixed. It can also show what content formats users are being offered.

6. Compare Backlinks and Referring Domains

Backlink analysis can show which pages and domains reference competing resources. Look for patterns rather than chasing a raw link count: which studies, guides, tools or news stories attract references, and why?

Use those findings to create genuinely linkable resources. Do not copy competitor link profiles or pursue manipulative placements just because a competitor has them.

See Link Building for SEO for a deeper explanation of sustainable link acquisition and link-spam risks.

7. Investigate Technical and On-Page Patterns

Compare important technical and page-level patterns such as crawlability, indexability, URL structure, internal linking, title clarity, headings, structured data and mobile usability.

The purpose is to identify useful patterns or weaknesses. Do not assume that every technical characteristic of a ranking competitor is a ranking requirement.

8. Turn Research Into a Content Gap

A real content gap exists when an important user need is insufficiently served by your current site and there is a credible opportunity to address it better.

GapPossible response
Missing topicCreate a new focused guide.
Weak existing sectionImprove the relevant page rather than creating a duplicate.
Outdated informationRefresh the existing resource with current evidence.
Different intentCreate a separate page only when the user task is materially different.
Overlapping URLsConsolidate or clarify the pages before publishing another similar URL.

9. A Practical SEO Competitor Analysis Workflow

  1. Select queries: choose representative terms tied to your business and content goals.
  2. Map competitors: record recurring ranking domains and page types.
  3. Classify intent: identify the user task behind each query.
  4. Analyze coverage: compare topics, entities, formats and content depth.
  5. Analyze links: inspect pages and assets that attract relevant references.
  6. Review technical patterns: check crawlability, architecture and page-level implementation.
  7. Find the gap: identify an unmet or weakly served user need.
  8. Choose the action: update, improve, consolidate or create based on evidence.

Common Competitor Analysis Mistakes

  • Copying competitor topics without understanding intent.
  • Assuming domain metrics prove content quality.
  • Treating third-party traffic estimates as exact facts.
  • Creating pages for every keyword gap even when intent is identical.
  • Chasing competitors' backlinks instead of building useful assets.
  • Assuming every ranking competitor's technical setup is a ranking requirement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SEO competitor analysis?

It is the process of studying pages and websites that compete for the same search demand to understand intent, keywords, content, links, technical patterns and opportunities.

Who are my SEO competitors?

Your SEO competitors are the pages or domains that repeatedly appear for the queries you care about. They may differ from your direct business competitors.

What should I analyze on a competitor website?

Review search intent, ranking pages, topics, content structure, backlinks, internal links, technical patterns, SERP features and signs of original value.

Should I copy what competitors are doing?

No. Competitor analysis is research. Use it to understand the search landscape and identify opportunities to provide a more useful, accurate or distinctive resource.

How often should SEO competitor analysis be done?

Run it when entering a new topic, launching important pages, seeing meaningful SERP changes or reviewing strategy. More frequent monitoring can be useful in fast-changing markets.
