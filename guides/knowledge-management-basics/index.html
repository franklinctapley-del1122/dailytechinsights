PRODUCTIVITY & KNOWLEDGE

What Is Knowledge Management?

Understand how organizations create, organize, preserve, find and share knowledge—and how software supports those processes.

Knowledge management is the practice of creating, organizing, storing, finding, sharing and maintaining knowledge so people can use it when they need it. Knowledge-management software provides digital structures for documents, policies, procedures, decisions, FAQs, research and other organizational information.

Why Does Knowledge Management Matter?

Important organizational knowledge often exists across documents, conversations, meetings, inboxes and individual employees. Without a repeatable system, information can become difficult to find, duplicated or outdated.

A knowledge-management approach makes information easier to capture, maintain and retrieve while clarifying who owns important content.

What Are the Main Types of Organizational Knowledge?

Explicit knowledge

Documented information such as policies, guides, specifications, reports and procedures.

Tacit knowledge

Experience, judgment and practical know-how that may be difficult to fully document.

Operational knowledge

Information needed to perform recurring processes, support customers or run systems.

Institutional knowledge

Historical context, decisions, lessons and organizational practices accumulated over time.

What Does a Knowledge Management System Include?

  • Centralized documents and pages
  • Search and navigation
  • Categories, tags or structured databases
  • Permissions and access controls
  • Version history and ownership
  • Templates for recurring documentation
  • Comments and collaboration
  • Integrations with communication and business systems

How Does a Knowledge Management Process Work?

  1. Capture: record useful information, decisions and lessons.
  2. Structure: organize content into a predictable information architecture.
  3. Validate: check accuracy, ownership and relevance.
  4. Publish: make approved knowledge accessible to the intended audience.
  5. Retrieve: help users find information through navigation and search.
  6. Maintain: review, update, archive or remove outdated content.

What Are Practical Examples of Knowledge Management?

Knowledge management becomes easier to understand when it is connected to everyday work. A support team might maintain troubleshooting steps, product answers and escalation procedures in a shared knowledge base. A human-resources team may organize policies, onboarding guidance and frequently asked questions. Product teams can document research findings, decisions, specifications and lessons from previous releases.

The goal in each case is similar: make useful organizational knowledge available beyond the person who originally created or learned it. This can reduce repeated questions and make established processes easier to follow, while still requiring people to review information for accuracy.

What Is the Difference Between a Knowledge Base and Knowledge Management?

A knowledge base is usually a specific collection of organized information, while knowledge management is the broader practice around creating, organizing, maintaining and sharing that information. A knowledge base can therefore be one part of a wider knowledge-management program.

This distinction matters because software alone does not create a reliable knowledge system. Teams also need clear ownership, useful content standards, review routines and an information structure that matches how people actually work.

How Can Teams Keep Knowledge Up to Date?

Assign ownership

Give important pages or knowledge areas a responsible owner who can coordinate updates and reviews.

Set review cycles

Review policies, procedures and frequently used guidance on a defined schedule or when processes change.

Use clear status signals

Show whether information is current, under review, archived or otherwise needs attention.

Connect knowledge to work

Link guidance to the workflows, projects, support processes and decisions where people actually use it.

What Should a Knowledge Management Strategy Include?

A practical strategy should define what knowledge is important, who owns it, where it should live and how users will find it. It should also establish basic rules for naming, categorization, permissions, review and archiving.

Start with a limited set of high-value information rather than attempting to document everything at once. After the structure is tested, teams can expand it based on real search behavior, recurring questions and gaps discovered during day-to-day work.

How Can Knowledge Management Support Collaboration?

Shared knowledge gives teams a common reference point when work crosses departments or when employees change roles. Instead of relying entirely on individual memory or repeated conversations, teams can refer to documented context, decisions and established procedures.

Collaboration still depends on communication and judgment. A knowledge-management system works best when it complements conversations and projects rather than trying to replace them.

What Makes Knowledge-Management Software Useful?

The most important capability is not simply storing more documents. A useful system makes trusted information easy to find and keeps ownership and maintenance visible. Search quality, information architecture, permissions and content governance therefore matter alongside editing features.

What Are Common Knowledge Management Problems?

  • Information scattered across too many tools
  • Duplicate or contradictory documents
  • Outdated procedures remaining discoverable
  • Unclear ownership
  • Poor search or navigation
  • Important decisions trapped in private conversations
  • Teams creating new documents instead of reusing existing knowledge

How Should You Choose Knowledge Management Software?

  1. Identify the knowledge people need most often.
  2. Map where that information currently lives.
  3. Define the required structure and permissions.
  4. Test realistic search and retrieval tasks.
  5. Check version history, ownership and content lifecycle controls.
  6. Evaluate integrations and data portability.
  7. Plan governance before migrating large volumes of content.

FAQs

Is a wiki a knowledge-management system?

A wiki can be used as a knowledge-management system, especially when teams need collaborative documentation and linked reference information.

Is Notion knowledge-management software?

Notion can support knowledge management through pages, databases, search, permissions and collaborative documentation.

What is the difference between knowledge management and document management?

Document management focuses heavily on controlling and organizing documents. Knowledge management is broader and includes the processes used to capture, contextualize, share and maintain organizational knowledge.
