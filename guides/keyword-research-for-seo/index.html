Keyword Research for SEO: What It Is & How It Works

Learn how keyword research helps you understand what people search for, evaluate demand and intent, prioritize opportunities and connect topics to useful pages.

Published: September 18, 2026 · Last reviewed: September 18, 2026 · By DailyTechInsights Editorial Team · See our methodology
Keyword research is the process of discovering and evaluating the search terms and questions people use when looking for information, products or solutions. For SEO, the goal is not to collect the largest possible list of phrases. It is to identify topics and queries that match your audience, business, content capability and search intent, then decide which deserve a page and how that page should serve the searcher. Current SEO guidance from Ahrefs and Semrush similarly frames keyword research around audience language, intent, metrics and prioritization.
Key idea: search volume is only one signal. A keyword with lower volume can be more useful when it has strong business relevance, clear intent and realistic competition for your site.

Why Is Keyword Research Important?

Keyword research helps translate audience problems into search topics you can serve. It can reveal the language people use, related questions, competing results, subtopics and opportunities for new or improved pages.

It also helps prevent two common mistakes: creating content around topics with little real demand, and targeting broad terms that do not match the page or the business goal. Keyword research should inform content planning, not replace editorial judgment.

How Keyword Research Works

  1. Start with a seed topic. Write down the product, problem, audience need or subject you want to understand.
  2. Generate keyword ideas. Use search results, autocomplete, related questions, competitor research, first-party data and keyword tools to expand the seed.
  3. Understand intent. Determine what the searcher appears to want: information, navigation, a product or a specific action.
  4. Group related queries. Combine close variants and subtopics when they can be served by the same page.
  5. Evaluate opportunity. Consider relevance, demand, competition, business value, current SERPs and your ability to create a useful result.
  6. Prioritize. Choose what to work on now, later or not at all.
  7. Map to content. Assign the topic or query group to an appropriate existing or new URL.

Ahrefs currently recommends generating keyword ideas, studying metrics and then prioritizing based on factors such as search intent, keyword difficulty, traffic potential and business potential.

Search Intent

Search intent is the likely purpose behind a query. Understanding it helps you decide what type of page should exist and what the user expects to see.

Intent patternWhat the searcher may wantLikely page type
InformationalAn explanation, definition or answer.Guide, explainer, glossary-style article or tutorial.
Commercial investigationResearch before choosing a product or approach.Comparison, alternatives page, evaluation guide or review.
TransactionalTo take an action such as purchase, sign-up or use a tool.Product, service or conversion-focused page.
NavigationalA specific brand, website or destination.Brand/product page or direct destination.

These labels are useful working categories rather than rigid rules. The actual search results are often the best evidence of what search engines currently interpret as relevant for a query.

Keyword Metrics Explained

MetricWhat it meansHow to use it carefully
Search volumeAn estimate of how often a query is searched over a period.Use it to understand demand, not as a guarantee of traffic or conversions.
Keyword difficultyA third-party estimate of ranking competition.Methodologies differ between vendors; compare it within the same tool and validate the actual SERP.
Traffic potentialAn estimate of how much organic traffic the topic or query could generate.Useful when deciding between closely related terms, but estimates vary by model and SERP.
Business potentialHow directly a topic connects to the problem a business solves.Keep it tied to your real product, audience and conversion path.
CPCA paid-search advertising metric that can provide competitive context.Do not treat CPC as an organic ranking signal or direct measure of SEO value.
SERP featuresElements such as snippets, local results, videos or other result formats.Check what page format users are being shown before choosing a content type.

Where to Find Keyword Ideas

  • Search-result observation: study autocomplete, related questions, result titles, recurring subtopics and the formats currently ranking.
  • First-party search data: use Search Console to understand queries already generating impressions and clicks for sites you manage.
  • Competitor research: inspect relevant competitor pages and the queries or topics they target, then look for gaps rather than copying them.
  • Keyword tools: use dedicated SEO software to expand seeds and analyze metrics at scale.
  • Customer language: sales calls, support questions, community discussions and product feedback can reveal valuable terminology that generic tools may miss.

Keyword tools can surface large numbers of ideas, but the output is only useful when it is interpreted in context. Ahrefs, for example, documents seed-keyword discovery, competitor research, related questions and multiple keyword metrics as parts of its workflow.

How to Prioritize Keywords

A practical prioritization model can combine five factors:

FactorQuestion
RelevanceDoes this topic closely match the audience and problem we serve?
Intent fitCan our page satisfy what the current search results suggest people want?
Business valueCould this topic contribute meaningfully to awareness, leads, sales or retention?
OpportunityIs there a realistic gap based on the current competition and our site's authority?
EffortCan we create and maintain a genuinely useful page at reasonable cost?

Do not turn these into a single universal score without understanding the business context. A new site, established brand, local business and global SaaS company can rationally prioritize different opportunities. Common Keyword Research Mistakes

  • Choosing keywords only because their search volume is high.
  • Treating third-party difficulty scores as exact predictions.
  • Ignoring the current SERP and search intent.
  • Creating separate pages for tiny keyword variations that have the same underlying intent.
  • Copying competitors instead of identifying the user need and adding something useful.
  • Building keyword lists without a publishing or content-mapping decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is keyword research in SEO?

It is the process of discovering and evaluating the words, phrases and questions people use in search, then deciding which topics are worth addressing based on intent, relevance, opportunity and business value.

What is the most important keyword metric?

There is no single most important metric for every project. Relevance and search intent are foundational, while volume, difficulty, traffic potential and business value help with prioritization.

Is high search volume always better?

No. High-volume queries can be broad, highly competitive or poorly aligned with the page and business. A lower-volume query with strong intent and business relevance may be more useful.

Do I need a paid keyword research tool?

Not necessarily. Search-result observation, Search Console, customer language and free tools can provide useful data. Paid platforms become more valuable when research volume and competitor analysis need to scale.

Should one page target one keyword?

Not as a rigid rule. One page can serve a group of closely related queries when they share the same underlying intent. Separate pages make more sense when the intent or user need is meaningfully different.
